# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project compares scripture and belief across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. That subject matter makes good conduct especially important: contributors and users come from, or care about, all three traditions (and none), and the project only works if it stays a place people can trust to be treated fairly regardless of their own beliefs.

We are committed to a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of religion (or lack of it), denomination, ethnicity, nationality, gender, sexual orientation, disability, age, or level of experience.

## Standards

Expected:

- Describe each tradition's beliefs and texts in terms adherents of that tradition would recognize as accurate, even where traditions disagree with one another.
- Disagree with a claim or a source, not with a person's faith or background.
- Cite sources for factual/textual claims where reasonably possible.
- Accept correction gracefully when a citation, translation, or characterization turns out to be wrong.

Not acceptable:

- Proselytizing, or using issues/PRs/discussions to argue that one tradition is correct and others are false, rather than to improve the project's content or code.
- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks — including ones directed at a religious group rather than an individual.
- Deliberately inserting inaccurate, unsourced, or one-sided claims about a tradition.
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Enforcement

Instances of unacceptable behavior may be reported to yusaftareen@gmail.com. Reports will be reviewed and addressed as appropriate to the circumstances — from a private word to removal from the project. Maintainers will respect the reporter's confidentiality.

## Scope

This applies within all project spaces (issues, PRs, discussions, code review) and when representing the project in public spaces.

# Contributor guide

Abrahamic Texts is a sourced introduction to Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Its one promise is that
**each tradition is described the way its own members would recognise it.** This guide explains how
anyone can help keep that promise, and how the project checks that it is kept.

## Ways to take part

| You want to… | Do this | Needs an account? |
|---|---|---|
| Point out a mistake or an unfair description | [Suggest a correction](/suggest) | No |
| Propose a new sourced claim | Sign in at [/account](/account), then **Propose a claim** | Yes (GitHub or Google) |
| Confirm that your own tradition is described fairly | Apply to review at [/account](/account) | Yes, and an administrator approves you |
| Fix code, tests or docs | Open a pull request (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md)) | A GitHub account |

## What happens to each contribution

```
Public report ──► editors read it ──► they may fix, or send the claim back for review
Proposal      ──► DRAFT (editors only) ──► IN REVIEW ──► tradition sign-off ──► PUBLISHED
```

* **Reports are input, not votes.** A hundred reports do not change anything by themselves, and a
  single careful one can. They never publish, edit or remove content on their own.
* **Proposals are drafts.** Only staff can see them. You cannot publish, and you cannot edit a
  proposal after sending it; if you spot a mistake, send a correction.
* **Nothing goes live without sign-off.** Before a claim is published, a member of *each tradition it
  describes* must confirm it is fairly described. The database refuses to publish it otherwise.

## Writing a good claim

A claim is **one sourced sentence about what a text or tradition says.**

1. **Attribute.** Say whose view it is: *"Jewish tradition holds that…"*, *"The Qur'an states that…"*,
   *"Many Christians read this as…"*. Never state a tradition's belief as plain fact.
2. **Describe, do not judge.** Do not say which tradition is right. Phrases such as *"the one true
   religion"*, *"obviously"*, *"everyone knows"* or *"so-called"* are turned away automatically.
3. **Cite the text.** Every claim needs at least one passage in its own source, written as
   `Book chapter:verse` (for example `Genesis 12:1-3`), up to 10 verses at a time. For the Qur'an,
   `2:255` works. A citation must come from the same source as the claim.
4. **One idea per claim.** Split it if you used "and" to join two different statements.
5. **Say where traditions differ.** If another tradition tells it differently, that is a second
   claim from that tradition's own source, not a correction inside the first.

You can send up to five proposals a day. If you already have 20 waiting, please wait for the editors.

## Becoming a reviewer

A reviewer answers one question about a claim: **"Would a thoughtful member of my tradition recognise
this as a fair description of it?"** They are not asked whether it is true.

* Apply for **your own tradition only**. You cannot review a tradition you do not belong to.
* Tell us the community, congregation, school or institution you belong to, and why you can speak to
  how your tradition is described. An administrator reads this; it is never published.
* **We cannot verify belief**, so we judge the community and the reason you give, and approvals can be
  revoked. One reviewer does not speak for a whole tradition, which holds many views; if you think a
  claim ignores a mainstream view of yours, say so in your review.
* You can **approve**, **ask for changes** or **object**. A change request or objection needs an
  explanation, which only the editors read.
* If you object and the editors publish anyway, the page says that a reviewer disagrees. That
  decision, with the reason, is recorded in the audit log.

### What the labels on a claim mean

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reviewed by a *X* reviewer | An approved member of that tradition confirmed the current wording |
| Checked by the project editor; awaiting a *X* reviewer | The administrator stood in so the claim could be published; **not** a review by that tradition |
| Awaiting *X* review | No one from that tradition has reviewed the current wording (this includes older material) |
| A *X* reviewer disagrees with this wording | A reviewer objected and the editors published it as contested |

Changing a claim's wording or its cited verses starts a new review, so these labels never describe
text that has since changed.

## Privacy

You can use the site, and send a correction, without an account. Accounts store your sign-in provider
ID, display name and email; you can delete your account at any time from your account page. Reports
store your message and optional email, plus a keyed hash of your IP address that is erased after 30
days. See the [privacy page](/privacy) for the full statement.

## Conduct

Be respectful of every faith and of every person. See [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
Contributions that demean a tradition or its members are removed, and accounts that persist can be
suspended.

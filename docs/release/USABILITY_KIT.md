# Five-person usability review kit

Status: ready to facilitate; no sessions or participant feedback have been recorded. Use the isolated demonstration preview. Do not use the production editorial account or real correction reports.

## Participants and setup

Invite five adults who are curious general readers: two new to comparative study, two with some familiarity, and one regular reader of religious history. This is a usability sample, not a panel representing entire faith communities. Recruitment and contact are the owner's responsibility. Offer keyboard-only participation and each person's preferred device. Include at least one phone-sized session; don't infer accessibility from a single participant.

Allow 25–30 minutes each. Assign P1–P5 instead of names. Ask for consent before taking notes; do not record audio, video, contact details or religious identity in this sheet. Provide a fresh browser profile with no saved progress and the same seeded preview for each person. Do not clear their personal browser data. Remind them that saves remain on this device. Stop if they want to stop.

## Neutral facilitator script

“We are testing the website, not your knowledge or beliefs. Please say what you are looking for and what you expect to happen. Some content is demonstration material awaiting editorial review. You may stop or skip any task. I will mostly observe; if something is unclear, that is useful for us to learn.”

Read one task at a time. Do not name buttons, demonstrate the answer, praise a particular interpretation, or explain navigation before an attempt. After 90 seconds of stalled progress ask “What would you try next?” At three minutes offer to move on. Record assistance separately from independent success. End with “What felt unclear?” and “What would you change first?” Avoid leading questions about whether the site felt premium.

## Tasks and observable success

| Task prompt | Independent success | Evidence to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Starting at the homepage, find a way to learn why Abraham appears in these traditions. | Opens the Abraham journey or a relevant published comparison and explains the chosen path. | First click, wrong turns, ability to distinguish journey from browsing. |
| 2. Find how two traditions describe Abraham's covenant. Explain which statements belong to which tradition. | Reaches a relevant comparison and correctly attributes two displayed positions without assuming they agree. | Tradition labels, understanding of project explanations, confidence versus evidence. |
| 3. Check the evidence for one statement. Show the exact passage it cites and identify the edition or project note. | Opens a citation at its highlighted verse, identifies its source/edition, and recognises a project note as an explanation. | Anchor visibility, numbering confusion, excerpt availability, return path. |
| 4. Keep that passage and its comparison so you can find them later. | Saves both; sees success or understands a storage failure without assuming a save succeeded. | Discoverability, feedback, accidental duplicate saves. |
| 5. Close this tab, reopen the same preview on this device, and return to what you kept. Remove one saved item. | Finds Saved, opens the expected destination, removes only the requested item, understands device-local persistence. | Recall, navigation, loss of context, misunderstanding of cloud sync. |

Do not count theological agreement or quiz scores as usability success. Record pass, assisted, partial, or fail per task. Stop the clock during network outages and label the interruption.

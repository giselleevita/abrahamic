## 1. Map place descriptions

10 places. Locations are approximate (shown on the page). Each has a story sentence and sometimes a caution.

### Ur
Eras: PATRIARCHAL · Links: /figures/abraham
> Genesis 11:28 names Ur of the Chaldeans as the place where Abraham’s family lived before he set out.
> Caution: Ur is traditionally placed in southern Iraq. Some Islamic and Jewish traditions name other birthplaces for Abraham.

### Haran
Eras: PATRIARCHAL · Links: /figures/abraham, /learn
> Terah’s family settles at Haran. In Genesis 12:1–4 Abraham is called to leave and go to a land he will be shown.

### Hebron and Canaan
Eras: PATRIARCHAL · Links: /figures/abraham, /figures/isaac, /figures/jacob
> Abraham lives in the land of Canaan, near Hebron. Genesis 23 records him buying a burial place there for Sarah.

### Egypt
Eras: PATRIARCHAL, EXODUS · Links: /figures/joseph, /figures/moses
> Genesis 37–50 tells how Joseph came to Egypt and how his family followed. Exodus 1–2 places the birth of Moses there.

### Sinai
Eras: EXODUS · Links: /figures/moses, /concepts/ten-commandments
> In the Torah, God gives the law to Moses at Sinai (Exodus 19–20). The Quran also speaks of Mount Tur, which Islamic tradition identifies with Sinai.
> Caution: The exact mountain is not certain; Jebel Musa is the traditional site.

### Jerusalem
Eras: KINGDOM, GOSPEL, EARLY_ISLAM · Links: /figures/david, /figures/solomon, /concepts/temple-jerusalem
> David makes Jerusalem his capital (2 Samuel 5) and Solomon builds the Temple. The Gospels place the last days of Jesus there. Islamic tradition holds that Muhammad’s night journey went to the “farthest mosque” (Quran 17:1), understood as Jerusalem.

### Nazareth
Eras: GOSPEL · Links: /figures/mary, /figures/jesus
> The Gospel of Luke (chapters 1–2) places Mary in Nazareth. The Quran tells her story in a chapter named for her, Maryam (chapter 19).

### Babylon
Eras: KINGDOM · Links: /figures/ezra
> After Jerusalem fell, many Judeans were taken to Babylon (2 Kings 25). The book of Ezra tells of a later return.

### Mecca
Eras: EARLY_ISLAM · Links: /figures/muhammad, /figures/ishmael
> According to Islamic tradition, Muhammad was born in Mecca and received the first revelations nearby. The Quran (2:127) speaks of Ibrahim and Ishmael raising the foundations of the Kaaba.

### Medina
Eras: EARLY_ISLAM · Links: /figures/muhammad
> In 622 Muhammad and his followers moved from Mecca to Medina, an event Muslims call the Hijra and the start of the Islamic calendar.

#### Many Names, One Story (AGE_6_8, seed status APPROVED, published: true)
> Some people appear in more than one holy book. The Torah calls a man Moshe. The Bible calls him Moses. The Quran calls him Musa. These are three names for one person. Each book tells his story in its own way. Sometimes the books agree. Sometimes these books tell a part differently. Reading them side by side helps us see both.

#### A Promise Under the Stars (AGE_6_8, seed status APPROVED, published: true)
> The Torah tells a story about a man named Abraham. One night he went outside his tent. He looked up at the night sky. There were more stars than he could count. The Torah says he was given a promise there. The Quran also tells about Abraham. It calls him Ibrahim. These books tell this part differently. Both of them remember him as important.

#### Three Books, One Family (AGE_9_12, seed status APPROVED, published: true)
> Judaism, Christianity, and Islam each have their own holy books. The Torah and the Hebrew Bible are central in Judaism. The New Testament is central in Christianity. The Quran is the holy book of Islam. These books are not the same, but they are related. Many of the same people appear across all three. Abraham appears in every one of them. So do Moses and many others. Sometimes the books describe an event in nearly the same way. Sometimes these books tell a story differently from each other. Sometimes one book mentions a person the others never name. Scholars read them side by side to see exactly where they match. This website does the same thing. It shows what each book says, and lets you compare them yourself.

#### A Boat and a Flood (AGE_6_8, seed status PENDING, published: false)
> The Torah tells a story about a man named Noah. He built a very large boat. The Torah says animals came aboard in pairs. Then a great flood covered the land. The Quran tells about Noah too. It calls him Nuh. These books tell this part differently. Both remember the boat and the water.
